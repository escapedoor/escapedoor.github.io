THE SECOND NIGHT

What you now know
  1. When a price is held below what buyers would pay (a price ceiling), more people want the thing than there is of it: a shortage. Then something else decides who gets it: time in a queue, the speed of a computer, luck, or who you know.
  2. A queue is not free. Its real price is the money and the hours, and the hours are paid but nobody receives them.
  3. Demand at a price counts everybody who would pay that price or more. A hall’s seats are a straight upright supply line, and the market-clearing price is where demand meets it.
  4. Price elasticity of demand: elastic means people buy much less when the price rises (there is a substitute); inelastic means nearly as much. A higher price brings in more money only while demand is inelastic, and even a fan has a limit.
  5. A gap between the face value and what buyers would pay is an incentive for touts. A rule changes the incentive, and it usually has unintended consequences as well.
  6. Consumer surplus is what you would have paid minus what you paid. A queue, a resale site, a high price and a draw each hand it to different people.
  7. Every way of sharing out a shortage has gainers and losers. A fair rule is one that says who they are, and can be defended out loud.
