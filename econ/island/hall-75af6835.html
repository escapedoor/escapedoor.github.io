What you now know
  1. Barter is swapping without money. It needs a double coincidence of wants: each person has to want what the other one has.
  2. Without that, you need a chain of swaps, and one link that fails breaks the whole chain. Money cuts any chain down to two swaps: sell, then buy.
  3. Money has three jobs: a way to pay (a medium of exchange), a measure of value (a unit of account) and a store of value.
  4. With one measure, prices can be compared at a glance, and a price that does not fit stands out.
  5. Good money is lasting, easy to carry, easy to divide, hard to fake, limited in supply and accepted by everyone. The last one is about people: it needs trust, and often a promise.
  6. A comparative advantage is in the thing you give up least to make. Two people can both gain from a swap even when one of them is better at everything.
  7. A swap helps both people when its price lies between their two opportunity costs.
