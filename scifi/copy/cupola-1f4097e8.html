IBEX AT THE IGLOO · 18.45

What you now know
  1. Light takes about 3 to 22 minutes to travel between Earth and Mars, one way, so there is no live conversation with Earth. An answer needs at least twice the light time. A reply that comes faster was not written on Mars.
  2. A sol, a day on Mars, is about 24 hours 40 minutes long. Mars clocks use slightly longer hours, so that noon stays at noon.
  3. Writers have habits they do not think about: small words like whilst and perhaps, spellings, short forms, punctuation, how they write the time, how long their sentences are. Count them in writing you know is theirs, then in the writing in doubt. One habit proves nothing; several together are evidence.
  4. The same person writes differently to a child, a friend, a boss and a log. That is register. A message can be in the right style for the wrong reader.
  5. A machine that copies a writer copies the surface: the greeting, the sign-off, the favourite word. It does not know what she knows, what has happened since, or what only two people share. It can be perfect in style and wrong in fact.
  6. The air on Mars is mostly carbon dioxide, and less than one hundredth as thick as ours: even a strong wind pushes gently. But a dust storm hides the Sun and can starve solar panels of power for weeks, as it did to the rover Opportunity in 2018.
  7. A message that does not sound like its writer is not always a fake. Hurry, cold hands or a different keyboard change how people write. Check the things that cannot change: what they know, and when the message could have come.

Say it aloud: explain to a friend, in five or six sentences, how you can tell whether a message was really written by the person whose name is on it.
