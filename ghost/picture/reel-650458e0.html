Till the Lights Go Up

The demolition men came at eight. They found the Bijou unlocked, the stalls empty, and every seat in row F turned up. Della Fraser was on the front steps with a flask of tea. She told them they could start whenever they liked.

What you now know
  • A film is a strip of still frames, sixteen to the foot. A projector stops each frame in the gate, hides the jerk with a shutter, and at sixteen or more pictures a second we see movement.
  • Silent pictures had no fixed speed (often 16 to 20 a second). Sound pictures run at exactly 24, because a sound track must run at an even speed.
  • The talkies came with The Jazz Singer (1927), and within a few years most picture houses had no more use for their pianists.
  • Old film was cellulose nitrate. It burns fiercely and makes its own oxygen, so projection boxes were fireproof, with iron shutters, fire traps and a film safe.
  • A picture came in reels. The operator changed from one machine to the other on two cue dots in the corner of the picture: the first to start the motor, the second to change over.
  • Title cards go in order by time words (by morning, meanwhile, years later), by tense (“had” reaches further back), by who speaks and by what each one answers. A flashback is a story inside the story.
  • “Used to” and “would” tell of old habits; and what a writer leaves out, like a name, can tell you as much as what he writes.
