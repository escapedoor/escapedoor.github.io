What you now know
  1. A plot is a mountain. Exposition: life before the trouble. Rising action: the climb, one step at a time. Climax: the moment the main problem is decided. Falling action: the loose ends. Resolution: the new normal.
  2. To find the climax, ask: after which moment can nothing go back to how it was?
  3. Every genre has its signs: the setting, the hero, the problem, the words on the first page. Books borrow each other’s coats, so decide by the main problem, not the setting.
  4. A first line is a promise and a hook. It can give you the voice, the world (and one thing that is wrong with it), the genre, and sometimes a whole character in one sentence.
  5. Some first lines are famous because they do all of that at once: “It was a bright cold day in April, and the clocks were striking thirteen.” (George Orwell, Nineteen Eighty-Four, 1949)
  6. Telling says what a character is like. Showing gives the evidence: what people do, say and choose, and what it costs them. From it you infer what they are really like.
  7. When the telling and the showing disagree, believe the showing. That is how you found out who really gave Hopwell its library.
