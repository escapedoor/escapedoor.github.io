What you now know
  1. A scale model shrinks every size and every distance by the same number. If the Sun were a ball 14 cm across, the Earth would be a pinhead 15 metres away, and the nearest star another ball 4,000 km away.
  2. An analogy explains something new with something the listener already knows. Choose it for your audience, and say where it breaks down: a misleading analogy is worse than none.
  3. If the 13.8 billion years of the universe were one year, the Sun and the Earth would form in early September, the first life would appear in late September, the first dinosaurs at Christmas, and all of written history would fit into the last 11 seconds of 31 December.
  4. A figure of speech can carry a true fact. “We are made of stardust” is a metaphor; the fact inside it is that, apart from hydrogen, nearly every atom in us was made in stars. Cut a figure that leaves a false picture: the Sun is not on fire.
  5. Some true facts sound like boasting: for a few weeks, one exploding star can shine as brightly as a whole galaxy. Check before you call a thing hyperbole.
  6. A good explanation begins with what the audience has seen, goes from small to large, asks its question before its answer, uses nothing before it has been given, and ends by coming home. True is not enough: a fact must move the explanation along.
  7. On 14 February 1990 Voyager 1 looked back from about 6 billion kilometres and photographed the Earth as a dot smaller than one pixel: the Pale Blue Dot.
