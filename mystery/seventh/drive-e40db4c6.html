PASSED FOR PRESS

What you now know
  1. A statement mixes what a person saw, what they heard and what they only assumed. Keep the three apart, in other people’s notes and in your own.
  2. In reported speech the verbs and the time words move one step back (“tomorrow” becomes “the next day”). Turn them back to find out who said what, to whom, and when.
  3. A lie is usually tidy and protects something. An honest mistake has a reason you can find: a dark landing, the wrong stairs, a voice through a wall.
  4. Must have means there is no other way; can’t have means it is ruled out; might have and could have mean it is possible but not proved. The opposite of must have is can’t have.
  5. A locked room is worked out from the keys, the windows, the plan and the times. If nobody could get in, the thing was never inside.
  6. The person with the best motive, who lied to you, may still be innocent of the thing you are looking for.
