THE WILL OF 5 MARCH 2019
STANDS

What you now know
  1. In England and Wales a will must be in writing and signed by the person making it, in front of two witnesses who are there at the same time; then each witness signs it.
  2. A later valid will cancels an earlier one. A letter or a card that nobody witnessed cannot make a will, or cancel one.
  3. If a witness, or a witness’s husband, wife or civil partner, is left something in the will, that gift fails, but the will still stands.
  4. The side that says a will is valid must prove it was properly made, on the balance of probabilities: more likely than not.
  5. A paper can give itself away: a day of the week that does not fit its date, a name that did not exist yet, a typewriter fault that came later. A carbon copy has every fault of the top copy.
  6. Who gains is a reason to check the evidence, not a proof that anybody lied. An honest witness can be wrong, and a liar can be partly right.
  7. An argument is built from parts joined by connectives: because, therefore, however, even if, which means that.
