Midnight. Hold up the tile, and say it to the Spring. How?

YEAR 412 · RE·SURG·ENT

What you now know
  1. A true name is built from parts: a PREFIX at the front says which way or how, a ROOT says what it has to do with, and a SUFFIX at the end says what kind of name it is.
  2. The suffix decides the kind: -OR, -ER the one that does it; -ION the doing of it; -ABLE can be done; -ENT, -ANT doing it or being it; -OUS full of; -ARIUM a place for.
  3. Read a name back into its parts to know what it will do, and to whom: SOMN·I·LUC·ENT shines sleep on you; DORM·ANT is only asleep itself.
  4. A whole word can mean more than its parts (an EQUINOX is a day, INFLUENZA is an illness), and two words can share a root and be unrelated things (a TERRIER and a TERRACOTTA tile).
  5. Some words only look as if they hold a root: LUCRATIVE is gain, not light; TERRIFIC is terror, not earth; SOLITAIRE is alone, not sun. One prefix can have two meanings: IL- is “not” in illegal and “on” in illuminate.
  6. A confident story about a word is not evidence. SINCERE does not come from “without wax”: check every claim against the oldest sources, and remember that a true part can sit in an untrue story.
  7. You can understand a dangerous name from its parts without ever saying it, and then do what is needed with other roots: rising (SURG) instead of flowing (FLU).
