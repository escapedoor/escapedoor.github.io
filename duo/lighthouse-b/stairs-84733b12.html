What you now know

  • Morse code turns letters into short and long flashes. A lamp call, AA AA AA, is answered with a row of long flashes, TTTT.
  • Every lighthouse has its own character, like Fl(3) 15s: groups of three flashes, repeated every 15 seconds. One turn of the lens takes the period times the number of faces.
  • A bearing taken from a ship and one taken from the shore point opposite ways: add or take away 180 degrees.
  • Two bearings of two lights make two lines on the chart, and the ship is where they cross: a fix.
  • Port is left and shows red; starboard is right and shows green. Red and green together: she is coming straight at you.
  • Ships and coastguards keep UTC, the same as GMT. British Summer Time is one hour ahead, so a tide table in GMT needs an hour added in summer.
  • Say numbers back figure by figure: fifteen is “one five”, because fifteen and fifty sound alike.
