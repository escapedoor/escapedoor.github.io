What you now know

  • The Blue Kite never left 9 Corn Market. For forty-seven years it was inside Mr Gorringe’s own portrait, behind his face.
  • He put the lights out himself, with the big main switch in his office. June Kitto heard it go clack, twice.
  • His statement could not be true: from the front window nobody can see St Aidan’s clock. It can only be seen from the back window, beside the switch.
  • He threw the empty frame down the cellar steps, so that Lewis Daintree would come up holding it.
  • Nearly six weeks before, the bank had refused him a loan. Three weeks before, he had raised the insurance to £15,000. Two weeks before, he had had his portrait’s frame made deeper.
  • June took her photograph in a mirror, so left and right in it are the wrong way round.
  • Places change and people change. Stone, windows and a scar do not: trust the things that cannot move.
