WHAT YOU NOW KNOW

  • Going up a river, your speed over the ground is your speed through the water minus the current, and a day’s run is speed times hours, with every stop counted.
  • At a bend the deep water is on the outside and a point bar lies on the inside; between bends the channel crosses over, and snags fall in from the cut bank.
  • Soundings are depths at one height of the river: correct them for today’s gauge, and remember that a channel is only as deep as its shallowest number.
  • On a river the boat coming down proposes how to pass: one short blast for left side to left side, two for right side to right side, five or more for danger or doubt, and one long blast at a bend you cannot see round.
  • A good reader of a journal sorts what the writer saw, what she thinks, what she worked out and what only seemed, and checks every conclusion against later facts, dates and distances.
  • A polite letter can be true in every line and still leave out what matters: read it against the facts, and always read the postscript.
  • Night flowers are pale and scented, for moths and bats; an epiphyte lives on a tree but does not feed on it; a label records what drying takes away; and a plant’s name has two parts, the genus with a capital letter and the species without.
