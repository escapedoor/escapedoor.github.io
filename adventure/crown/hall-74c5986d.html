WHAT YOU NOW KNOW

  • Read a rota exactly: the time a round begins, how long it lasts, and the gap before the next one — and do the arithmetic on a 24-hour clock.
  • A sweeping camera is blind for most of its cycle; work out the minutes it rests on a spot, and cross only when it is looking away.
  • A route works both ways: you can follow one from exact words (along, past, through, opposite, the first door on your right) and you can give one.
  • Three accounts of one night seldom agree; lay them on one timeline, and sort what each person saw, was mistaken about, or was covering for — checking every claim against a fixed fact.
  • The first, obvious story can be the wrong one; throw it out when plainer facts (a fast watch, a cleaner’s trolley, a light left on) explain the night better.
  • When a plan breaks half way, say the new one in conditionals — if, unless, in case, otherwise — built from what has just gone wrong.
  • Some locks need two earlier rooms at once: the camera’s rule and the guards’ rota together gave you the one safe minute.
