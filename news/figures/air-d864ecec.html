ON AIR 20.52 · CORRECTED 08.00

What you now know
  1. A rate that goes from 4% to 6% has risen by two percentage POINTS, and by 50 per cent. “Up two per cent” is neither.
  2. A per cent change is measured from where it started. On a small base, two burglaries becoming three, a big per cent can be one single thing.
  3. The mean adds up and divides; the median is the one in the middle. One huge number pulls the mean up and hardly moves the median.
  4. To compare places, or years, of different sizes, use a rate (per person, per 1,000 people), not a total.
  5. Check where a graph’s side axis starts and which years it shows. A survey speaks only for the people it asked: too few, or people who chose themselves, cannot speak for a town.
  6. Two things can rise together without one causing the other: look for the same rise where the supposed cause was missing.
  7. Play the whole answer before you quote it, report it with a fair verb and the tense moved back (will → would), give every verdict one sentence of reason, use the same rules for both sides, and correct your own mistakes plainly.
